🌸 How Do Organisms Reproduce: Comprehensive NCERT Notes

From asexual modes and flower pollination to human gametogenesis and reproductive health.

Academic Session 2025–2026 | NCERT Aligned
Home › Study Materials Hub › Reproduction in Organisms
🎯 Core Diagram: Longitudinal Section (LS) of a Flower and the Female Reproductive System are among the most frequently tested diagram questions in Class 10 board exams.

1. Modes of Asexual Reproduction with Examples

Asexual reproduction involves a single parent without the formation or fusion of gametes, producing genetically identical offspring:

  • Fission:
    • Binary Fission: Parent organism splits into two equal daughters in any plane (e.g., Amoeba) or in a fixed longitudinal plane (e.g., Leishmania, which has a whip-like flagellum).
    • Multiple Fission: Parent cell divides repeatedly inside a cyst under unfavorable conditions, releasing multiple daughter cells simultaneously (e.g., Plasmodium, the malaria parasite).
  • Fragmentation: Multicellular organisms with simple cellular organization break into mature pieces upon maturation (e.g., Spirogyra filaments).
  • Regeneration: If chopped into pieces, specialized cells proliferate to regenerate missing organ systems (e.g., Planaria, Hydra). (Regeneration is not reproduction per se, but an accidental repair mechanism).
  • Budding: Outgrowth develops due to repeated cell division at one specific site, detaching when fully mature (e.g., Hydra, Yeast).
  • Spore Formation: Spores produced in globular structures called sporangia; protected by thick walls until contacting moisture (e.g., Rhizopus bread mold).
  • Vegetative Propagation: New plants arise from vegetative units like roots (Sweet potato), stems (Potato eye tubers, Ginger rhizome), or leaves (Bryophyllum leaf notches).

2. Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants (Angiosperms)

Flowers represent the reproductive organs of angiosperm plants. A complete bisexual flower possesses four concentric whorls:

  • Calyx (Sepals): Outermost green whorl that protects flower in bud stage.
  • Corolla (Petals): Brightly colored, scented petals that attract insect pollinators.
  • Androecium (Stamen - Male organ): Comprises anther (produces haploid pollen grains) and supporting filament.
  • Gynoecium / Pistil (Carpel - Female organ): Comprises three parts:
    • Stigma: Sticky terminal surface that receives pollen grains.
    • Style: Elongated tubular neck through which pollen tube descends.
    • Ovary: Basal swollen chamber containing ovules, each harboring a female egg cell.

Double Fertilization Process:

Upon pollination, pollen germinates on stigma and sends a pollen tube carrying two male gametes into the ovule:

  1. Syngamy: One male gamete fuses with the female egg cell to form a diploid Zygote (which develops into the plant embryo).
  2. Triple Fusion: The second male gamete fuses with two polar nuclei to form triploid Primary Endosperm Nucleus (which develops into nutritive endosperm tissue).

Post-fertilization: The ovule develops a tough seed coat, while the ovary ripens and enlarges into the fruit.

3. Human Reproductive Systems & Gametogenesis

Male Reproductive System:

  • Testes: Located outside abdominal cavity in the scrotum because sperm formation requires a temperature 2–2.5°C lower than normal internal body core temperature. Also secretes Testosterone.
  • Vas Deferens: Tube transporting mature sperm from testes to urethra.
  • Accessory Glands (Prostate & Seminal Vesicles): Add lubricating, alkaline secretions rich in fructose to nourish sperm and ease transit (forming Semen).

Female Reproductive System:

  • Ovaries: Produce one mature ovum alternately every 28 days and secrete hormones Estrogen and Progesterone.
  • Fallopian Tube (Oviduct): Site of fertilization where sperm meets ovum. Cilia sweep zygote toward uterus.
  • Uterus (Womb): Elastic muscular bag where embryo implants into thickened vascularized endometrium.
  • Placenta: Disc-shaped vascular organ embedded in uterine wall. Contains villi on embryo side and blood spaces on maternal side, providing glucose, oxygen, and removing urea waste.

4. Reproductive Health & Contraceptive Methods

Contraceptive methods prevent unwanted pregnancies and curb Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs):

  • Barrier Methods: Condoms, diaphragms, and cervical caps prevent physical union of sperm and egg. Condoms uniquely prevent transmission of bacterial STIs (Gonorrhea, Syphilis) and viral STIs (HIV/AIDS, Genital Warts).
  • Chemical / Hormonal Methods: Oral contraceptive pills alter hormonal balance to stop ovulation. May cause hormonal side effects.
  • Intrauterine Devices (IUDs): Copper-T placed inside uterus by healthcare professionals; releases copper ions that suppress sperm motility and fertilization capacity.
  • Surgical Methods (Permanent):
    • Vasectomy: In males, a small portion of vas deferens is cut and ligated, preventing sperm from entering semen.
    • Tubectomy: In females, a small portion of fallopian tubes is cut and ligated, preventing ovum from reaching uterus.

💡 Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

❓ What happens when the egg is not fertilized in a human female?

If the ovum is not fertilized, progesterone levels drop, and the thick, spongy uterine endometrium lining prepared for receiving the zygote breaks down. Blood and mucus tissues are discharged through the vagina in a cyclic process lasting 3–5 days called Menstruation, which recurs approximately every 28 days.

❓ Why is DNA copying an essential part of the reproduction process?

DNA contains the fundamental genetic instructions for assembling cellular proteins and creating body design. Accurate DNA replication ensures that offspring inherit the species-specific body plan. Minor biochemical variations during replication introduce genetic diversity, which is critical for evolutionary survival amid fluctuating environmental conditions.
