⚖️ Power Sharing: Case Studies, Models & Constitutional Forms

Comparing Belgium’s prudent accommodation with Sri Lanka’s majoritarian conflict.

Academic Session 2025–2026 | NCERT Aligned
Home › Study Materials Hub › Power Sharing (Civics)
💡 Prudential vs Moral Reasons: Prudential reasons stress that power sharing prevents violent social conflict and maintains political stability; Moral reasons emphasize that power sharing is the very spirit of true democracy.

1. Ethnic Demographics: Belgium vs Sri Lanka

Belgium (European Country):

  • 59% of population lives in Flemish region and speaks Dutch.
  • 40% of population lives in Wallonia region and speaks French.
  • 1% speaks German.
  • Capital Brussels Inversion: In Brussels, 80% speak French while 20% speak Dutch. The French minority was historically wealthy and politically dominant, causing intense friction with the Dutch majority.

Sri Lanka (Island Nation):

  • 74% Sinhala speakers (mostly Buddhist).
  • 18% Tamil speakers (13% Sri Lankan Tamils, 5% Indian origin Tamils; Hindu and Muslim).
  • 7% Christians (both Sinhala and Tamil).

2. Sri Lankan Majoritarianism vs Belgium Accommodation

Majoritarianism in Sri Lanka (1956 Act):

Sri Lanka gained independence in 1948. Sinhala leaders adopted majoritarian policies:

  • Passed an Act in 1956 declaring Sinhala as the sole official language, disregarding Tamil.
  • Preferential university admissions and government job policies favoring Sinhala applicants.
  • State committed to foster and protect Buddhism as official state religion.
  • Result: Tamils felt alienated and demanded an independent state (Tamil Eelam). Escalated into a devastating civil war causing thousands of deaths and economic ruin.

Belgium’s Innovative Accommodation Model (Amended 4 Times between 1970–1993):

  • Constitution mandates equal number of Dutch and French ministers in central government.
  • Many central powers transferred permanently to autonomous state governments (not subordinate to center).
  • Brussels has a separate government with equal representation for both language communities.
  • Unique Community Government elected by people belonging to one language community (Dutch, French, German) to decide cultural, educational, and linguistic affairs.
  • Result: Avoided civic strife and partition. Brussels was chosen as the headquarters of the European Union.

3. The Four Forms of Power Sharing in Modern Democracies

Power is distributed across multiple dimensions in a constitutional democracy:

  1. Horizontal Distribution of Power: Shared among different organs of government at the same level — Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary. Creates a system of Checks and Balances so no branch wields absolute power.
  2. Vertical Distribution (Federal Division): Shared among governments at different territorial tiers — Central Government, State Government, and Local Panchayats/Municipalities.
  3. Power Sharing Among Social Groups: Reserved constituencies for scheduled castes/tribes and women, protecting religious and linguistic minority voices.
  4. Power Sharing Among Political Parties & Pressure Groups: Multi-party coalitions (where parties form alliance governments) and interest groups (trade unions, farmers, business associations) influencing policy decisions.

💡 Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

❓ Differentiate between horizontal and vertical power sharing.

Horizontal power sharing distributes authority among equal organs of government (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary) operating at the same administrative tier, implementing checks and balances. Vertical power sharing divides authority hierarchically between higher and lower governmental levels (Union, State, Local bodies) via constitutional federalism.

❓ Why is power sharing desirable in a democracy? Give two reasons.

(1) Prudential Reason: It prevents social tension and violent conflict among diverse ethnic communities, ensuring smooth political stability; (2) Moral Reason: Democracy by definition involves sharing power with all citizens affected by governance decisions; legitimate power rests on the consent of the governed.
