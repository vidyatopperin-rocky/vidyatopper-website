1. What is Federalism & Key Features

Federalism is a system of governance in which sovereign authority is shared between a central national authority and constituent political units (provinces or states).

7 Core Features of Federalism:

  • Two or more tiers of government governing the same citizens, but each tier has its own defined jurisdiction in legislation, taxation, and administration.
  • Constitutional jurisdiction guarantees: Existence and authority of each tier are constitutionally entrenched.
  • Fundamental constitutional provisions cannot be unilaterally altered by one tier; requires consent of both central and state legislatures.
  • Judiciary acts as an umpire: Supreme Court resolves jurisdictional disputes between different tiers of government.
  • Financial autonomy: Sources of revenue for each level are clearly specified to ensure financial independence.

2. "Coming Together" vs "Holding Together" Federations

  • Coming Together Federations: Independent sovereign states come together voluntarily to form a larger federal union, pooling sovereignty while retaining individual identity to increase collective security. All constituent states typically possess equal powers.
    Examples: USA, Switzerland, Australia.
  • Holding Together Federations: A large sovereign country divides its administrative power between national and provincial governments. Central government tends to be more powerful than states, and some constituent units may enjoy asymmetrical powers (special autonomy).
    Examples: India, Spain, Belgium.

3. Three-Fold Legislative Distribution (The 7th Schedule)

The Indian Constitution divides lawmaking jurisdiction into three distinct lists:

Constitutional List Lawmaking Jurisdiction Core Subjects
Union List (~100 subjects) Exclusively Parliament (Central Government) National defense, foreign affairs, atomic energy, banking, communications, and currency.
State List (~61 subjects) Exclusively State Legislative Assemblies Police, public health, sanitation, agriculture, prisons, and local trade.
Concurrent List (~52 subjects) Both Parliament and State Assemblies. (In case of conflict, Central law prevails). Education, forests, trade unions, marriage, adoption, and succession.
Residuary Subjects Exclusively Union Parliament Matters arising after the constitution was framed, e.g., Information Technology, cyber laws, and space commerce.

4. Decentralization & The Historic 1992 Amendment

Decentralization takes power away from central and state governments and delegates it to local grassroots bodies (Panchayats and Municipalities). The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992) transformed local governance:

  • Constitutionally mandatory to hold regular elections to local government bodies every 5 years.
  • Seats reserved in elected bodies and executive posts for Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), and Other Backward Classes (OBC).
  • At least one-third (33%) of all seats and positions are strictly reserved for women.
  • An independent State Election Commission created in each state to conduct panchayat and municipal elections.
  • State governments are mandated to share specified revenues and taxes with local governance bodies.

💡 Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

❓ Why did the makers of our Constitution reject Hindi as the sole national language of India?

Hindi is the mother tongue of only about 44% of Indians. Imposing Hindi would have triggered violent separatist linguistic protests (as seen in Tamil Nadu in 1965). The Constitution avoided declaring any single national language, instead recognizing Hindi as the official language along with 21 other Scheduled Languages in the 8th Schedule, preserving national unity.

❓ Explain the structure of the Panchayati Raj system at the rural level.

Gram Panchayat functions at the village level with ward members and a Sarpanch elected by the Gram Sabha (all adult voters). A few Gram Panchayats are grouped together to form a Panchayat Samiti or Block/Mandal. All Panchayat Samitis in a district constitute the Zilla Parishad, headed by a politically elected Zilla Parishad Chairperson.
